# RFC 0001: The SalahTimes API

- Status: accepted for 0.1.0, open to findings from SalahKit's SalahCalculation and its consumers
- Created: 2026-10-13

## Context

SalahTimes is the salah-kit ecosystem's prayer-time engine. It replaces adhan-swift (salah-kit `proposal/AMENDMENTS.md`, Amendment 3) and is written from published astronomy and each method's published definition. Any app can use it alone. SalahKit's `SalahCalculation` product adapts it to SalahCore's `PrayerSource`, whose contract (proposal §7.1) asks a source for one civil day in the location's time zone and expects:

- typed events whose owning day can differ from the civil day of their instant, such as an Isha after midnight;
- explicit unavailability instead of a substituted or invented time;
- `Sendable` values that a widget extension can compute and cache.

This RFC records the decisions behind the 0.1.0 API. The computation itself is specified in [`docs/algorithm.md`](../algorithm.md).

## Decisions

### 1. A civil day in a time zone goes in; instants come out

```swift
let times = try PrayerTimes(
  coordinates: Coordinates(latitude: 21.4225, longitude: 39.8262),
  day: GregorianDay(year: 2026, month: 3, day: 20)!,
  timeZone: TimeZone(identifier: "Asia/Riyadh")!,
  parameters: CalculationMethod.ummAlQura.parameters)
```

- **Why a civil day and a time zone.** That's what SalahCore asks a source for. The time zone only selects the solar day (the transit nearest the civil day's local noon, algorithm §5), which is what makes places near the date line, such as Kiritimati at UTC+14, get the right day. The engine never formats anything in it.
- **Why instants.** Callers own time zones, calendars and display. A `Date` is unambiguous across DST, and a widget can cache it.
- **Why `GregorianDay`.** Foundation's `DateComponents` carries optional fields, a calendar and a time zone that the engine would have to validate or ignore. `GregorianDay` is a small validated value: its failable initializer rejects 30 February, its `Codable` form is the ISO 8601 date the vectors use, and `GregorianDay(containing:in:)` gives "today" in a zone. The name avoids SalahCore's `CivilDay`, so a module that imports both needs no qualification.

### 2. The owning day is the day you asked for

Every event in a `PrayerTimes` value belongs to its `day`, even when its instant falls on a neighboring civil day:
- Isha after midnight (high latitudes in summer; places whose clock runs far ahead of the Sun, such as Kashgar on China Standard Time);
- the middle of the night and its last third, which usually fall after midnight;
- rarely, a Fajr bounded by a high-latitude rule just before the day begins, at high-latitude places east of their time zone's meridian.

The engine doesn't reorder or move events between days. An adapter that needs an owning-day offset compares each instant with the civil day's bounds in the zone.

### 3. Events that don't occur are typed, never invented

Each event is an `EventTime`: `.occurs(Date)` or `.doesNotOccur(NonOccurrence)`. The two reasons say which way the Sun failed: `sunStaysAbove` (no sunset in polar day, no Isha at 18° in a white night) and `sunStaysBelow` (no sunrise in polar night). An event defined from another one, such as Isha by interval or the middle of the night, takes the reason of the event it depends on. Two reasons are enough for an app to explain the gap, and every other language can reproduce them.

Invalid input is a different thing: the initializer throws `CalculationError` (`invalidCoordinates`, `invalidParameters`, `unsupportedDate`).

### 4. Parameters are typed rules

`CalculationParameters` holds a `FajrRule`, `MaghribRule` and `IshaRule` instead of adhan-swift's flat fields (an angle, an interval that wins when it's positive, a separate shafaq):

| Rule | Cases |
|---|---|
| `FajrRule` | `angle(Double)`, `moonsightingCommittee` |
| `MaghribRule` | `sunset`, `angle(Double)` |
| `IshaRule` | `angle(Double)`, `minutesAfterMaghrib(Int)`, `moonsightingCommittee(Shafaq)` |

- Combinations that mean nothing, such as an Isha angle and an interval at once, can't be expressed.
- The Moonsighting Committee's seasonal behavior is a rule, not a check of which preset the parameters came from. A custom method can combine it with Hanafi Asr, as the committee's own London timetable does.
- The `Codable` form is the JSON in `Vectors/`, so a vector's parameters decode directly.

The other fields: `madhab`, `highLatitudeRule`, `nightSpan`, `methodAdjustments`, `adjustments`, `rounding` and `ramadanIsha`. Decoding requires only `fajr` and `isha`; every other key has a default, so settings saved by an older version keep decoding when a field is added.

The parameters don't record which preset they came from. An app that shows "Muslim World League (customized)" stores its own choice.

### 5. Methods are presets of parameters

`CalculationMethod` lists the published methods; `method.parameters` returns their values. Names follow the authority where it's the usual name (`diyanet`, `muis`, `jakim`, `kemenag`) and the country otherwise (`dubai`, `kuwait`, `qatar`). `Vectors/methods.json` holds the same values, so engines in other languages share them, and a test keeps the two identical.

Method adjustments, the minutes an authority adds for precaution, sit in `methodAdjustments`, apart from the user's `adjustments`, so changing one never erases the other.

A preset reproduces its authority's times. When the authority documents its rules, the preset follows them: its angles or intervals, and the precautionary minutes and rounding it documents; minutes its timetables add beyond those rules stay out. When the authority publishes only timetables (MUIS, the UAE), the preset is fitted to them, minutes and rounding included. Every value is documented or measured, and METHODS.md gives the source or the fit for each, and the differences measured against official timetables.

### 6. High latitudes

- The rules are `middleOfTheNight`, `seventhOfTheNight`, `twilightAngle`, `disabled`, and `recommended`, the default in every preset.
- `recommended` resolves by absolute latitude: `seventhOfTheNight` beyond 48°, north or south, and `middleOfTheNight` elsewhere. Southern high latitudes such as Ushuaia work by design.
- A rule bounds Fajr and Isha by a portion of the night measured from sunset to sunrise, and replaces the angle's time only when that time is beyond the bound or doesn't exist.
- **Each prayer uses its own night.** Fajr uses the night that ends at its sunrise, and Isha the night that starts at its sunset. Using one night for both, as adhan-swift does, can put a bounded Fajr before the previous day's bounded Isha when nights lengthen quickly (adhan-swift issue #102 reports out-of-order times at high latitudes). With each prayer's own night, the middle-of-the-night rule makes Isha and the next Fajr meet at the same instant instead of crossing.
- `disabled` exists for apps that would rather say "Isha doesn't occur tonight" and apply their own policy.
- Interval-based Isha is never bounded, matching how the methods that use it publish it.

### 7. Opt-ins live in the engine

Both are off by default and documented as policy (proposal §4, principle 7):

- **Ramadan Isha.** `RamadanIshaRule.interval(minutes:hijriDayOffset:)` replaces a positive Isha interval on days that fall in Ramadan in the Umm al-Qura calendar, after shifting the day by the Hijri offset. It touches nothing else and never changes angle-based Isha (adhan-swift issue #23 explains why this can't be automatic).
- **Country suggestions.** `CalculationMethod.suggested(forCountryCode:)` and `suggestionsByCountryCode` give a sourced starting point. Turkey suggests Diyanet, Malaysia JAKIM, Indonesia Kemenag and Singapore MUIS. A country without a sourced suggestion returns `nil`; the app decides.

SalahCalculation wraps these; it doesn't re-implement them.

### 8. Value types everywhere

Every public type is a `Sendable`, `Codable`, `Hashable` value, except the `Qibla` namespace and the `CalculationError` error. The engine has no global mutable state, so a widget extension or a background task can compute concurrently.

### 9. Qibla

`Qibla.direction(from:)` returns the great-circle initial bearing to the Kaaba in degrees from true north, or `nil` for invalid coordinates and at the Kaaba or its antipode.

### 10. Platforms

iOS 13, macOS 10.15, watchOS 6, tvOS 13 and visionOS 1, plus Linux. The engine needs only `Foundation`'s `Date`, `TimeZone` and, for the Ramadan rule, the Umm al-Qura `Calendar`. Swift tools 6.0, Swift 6 language mode.

## Deliberately not included

- **Observer elevation.** Few published methods use it; adding it later is additive.
- **"Nearest latitude" and "nearest day" estimation** for places where twilight lasts for months. Apps can use `disabled` and apply them.
- **Hijri dates, names, localization and formatting.** SalahCore owns words and display.
- **Location services.** Callers pass coordinates and a time zone.
- **Imsak, sunrise prayer windows and prohibited times.** None has a consumer yet.

## Questions for SalahCalculation (session B1)

- Does SalahCore's chronology validation accept a Fajr whose instant precedes its owning day (decision 2, third case)? If not, the adapter should report that day as partial or unavailable rather than move the event.
- Should SalahCalculation expose `nightSpan`, the night's divisions and `Qibla`, or leave them to apps that import SalahTimes directly?
